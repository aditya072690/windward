Methodology

How a vintage is scored

Cutoff is the moment a forecast is published. Everything after it is scored against metered outturn; everything before it is observed history and is not credited to the forecast.

Forecast is the published half-hourly series for the selected vintage. Actual is the metered series. The shaded area between them is the error — green where the forecast ran low, red where it ran high.

Conservative line

The dashed line adds a per-series risk margin above the central forecast. It is a planning aid, not a confidence interval, and moves with the series: wider for wind, tighter for solar.

The three numbers

MAE averages the absolute miss across every scored half-hour. Bias averages the signed miss, so a positive value means the forecast ran high. Accuracy is 100 minus weighted absolute percentage error, which keeps the figure readable on low-demand nights.

Percentiles

P50, P90 and P95 summarise the distribution of absolute error inside each lead-time bucket. Read them together: a stable P50 with a widening P95 is a forecast that is usually right and occasionally very wrong.

All values here are illustrative, covering 1–31 January 2026 in Europe/London. Nothing on this page is an operational forecast.
